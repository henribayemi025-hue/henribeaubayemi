10 minutes a day to know where I stand

For a shop, a pharmacy, a garage, a workshop, a restaurant. Use it with the kit's workbook — a notebook works too at the start.

Morning 3 minutes

  1. Write the date and count your opening float, the money you open with. → Daily cash
  2. Look at the “Reorder?” column: what is running out gets ordered today, not when the shelf is empty. → Items
  3. A delivery arrives? One row before you shelve it, and “Paid?: No” for as long as you owe the money. → Stock received

Evening 7 minutes

  1. Copy in the day's sales if you jotted them in a notebook. → Daily sales
  2. Count notes and coins, write the counted cash.
  3. Read the difference. Zero: well done. Otherwise, look now, while the day is fresh: wrong change, an expense not written down, a forgotten sale. Write the explanation.
  4. Check the mobile money sales against the messages on your phone.
  5. A glance at the Summary: sales, margin, what is running out.

During the day, and once a week

  • Every sale, one row. Every note that leaves the till for anything other than a sale, one row in “Cash out” — including the one you take home.
  • Once a week, count five random items on the shelf and compare with the “Stock” column. A difference that keeps coming back means breakage, theft or an unrecorded sale.

The 5 mistakes that lose money

  1. Taking from the till without writing it down. Household money and business money get mixed, and you no longer know if you are making a profit.
  2. Pricing without your true purchase cost. Transport, packaging, taxes: if they are left out, the margin you see is wrong.
  3. Giving credit on a handshake. A name, an amount, a date — or the money does not come back.
  4. Ordering by guesswork. Too much of what sits, not enough of what sells. That is what the reorder level is for.
  5. Counting the cash “when there's time”. A one-day difference can be explained; a one-month difference never.

When to move to a tool

The workbook is enough to get started. It becomes too small when:

  • copying the notebook takes longer than selling;
  • several people run the till;
  • your customers ask for a receipt;
  • you need to know who owes you what, and since when;
  • you are asked for figures you rebuild by hand.
